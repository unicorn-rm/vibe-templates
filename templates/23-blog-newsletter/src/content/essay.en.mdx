Nobody reads a book in one sitting any more, and that is fine. What changed is not the length of our attention but the number of things asking for it.<Fn n={1}>A 2024 survey found the average reader opens a novel nine times before page fifty. Most of those openings last under four minutes.</Fn> A paperback has no notifications. It cannot update itself while you sleep.

The trick, I think, is to make a place for reading rather than a time. A chair that is only for books. A bag pocket that always has one in it. A tram line long enough for a chapter.<Fn n={2}>Line 7 in Riga, end to end: forty-one minutes, or roughly one Chekhov story.</Fn>

## The margin is the point

Old books were written to be argued with. Readers filled the margins with objections, little drawings, shopping lists. Those notes are why second-hand copies are worth more than new ones.

This newsletter is an attempt to keep a margin alive. Each Sunday: one essay, three books, and the best note a reader sent back that week.<Fn n={3}>Replies go to a real inbox and are read by a real person, usually on Sunday evening with tea.</Fn>

> A book is a machine to think with.
> — I. A. Richards
